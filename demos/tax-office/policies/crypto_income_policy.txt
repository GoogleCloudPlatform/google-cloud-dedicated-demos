CRYPTOCURRENCY INCOME DECLARATION POLICY

Section 1: Cryptocurrency Income Reporting Requirements

All taxpayers who hold cryptocurrency accounts on exchanges such as Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, Bitpanda, or any other digital asset platform are required to report any income generated from cryptocurrency transactions.

Section 2: Definition of Cryptocurrency Income

Cryptocurrency income includes but is not limited to:
- Capital gains from selling, trading, or exchanging cryptocurrencies
- Mining rewards and staking rewards
- Airdrops and forks
- Interest earned from lending or yield farming
- Payment received in cryptocurrency for goods or services

Section 3: Zero Declaration Violations

Taxpayers who maintain verified cryptocurrency exchange accounts but declare zero cryptocurrency income are considered high-risk for tax evasion. This is especially true if:
- The exchange account shows transaction activity during the tax year
- The account has been verified for trading (KYC completed)
- The taxpayer has a history of cryptocurrency transactions in previous years

Section 4: Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failure to accurately report cryptocurrency income may result in:
- Tax assessments with penalties of up to 25% of the undeclared amount
- Interest charges on unpaid taxes
- Criminal prosecution in cases of willful tax evasion
- Fines ranging from €5,000 to €50,000 depending on the severity

Section 5: Record Keeping Requirements

Taxpayers must maintain detailed records of all cryptocurrency transactions for at least 10 years, including:
- Transaction dates and times
- Purchase and sale prices in local currency
- Exchange fees and transaction costs
- Wallet addresses and exchange account statements
- Records of private key transfers

Section 6: Exchange Data Sharing

Tax authorities have agreements with major cryptocurrency exchanges to share taxpayer data. Non-compliance will be detected through automated systems that cross-reference exchange data with tax declarations.

Section 7: Voluntary Disclosure

Taxpayers who have failed to report cryptocurrency income in previous years are encouraged to make voluntary disclosures. Reduced penalties may apply for voluntary compliance before detection.
