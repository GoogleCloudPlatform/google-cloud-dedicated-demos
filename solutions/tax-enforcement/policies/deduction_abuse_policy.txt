DEDUCTION ABUSE AND HIGH DEDUCTION RATIO POLICY

Section 1: Normal Deduction Ranges

Statistical analysis shows that legitimate deduction ratios typically fall within these ranges:
- Individual taxpayers: 15-35% of gross income
- Small businesses: 25-45% of gross income
- Large corporations: 30-50% of gross income

Deduction ratios exceeding 50% of gross income trigger automatic review procedures.

Section 2: Common Deduction Abuse Patterns

High-risk deduction patterns include:
- Home office expenses exceeding 20% of rent/mortgage
- Vehicle expenses without supporting business use documentation
- Business meal deductions exceeding €50 per person per meal
- Education expenses unrelated to current employment
- Excessive charitable donations (over 20% of income)
- Medical expenses without proper receipts and prescriptions
- Work equipment purchases inconsistent with profession

Section 3: Business Expense Documentation Requirements

All business expense deductions require:
- Original receipts or electronic records
- Clear business purpose documentation
- Proof of payment (bank statements, credit card statements)
- Separation of private and business use for mixed-use items
- Logbooks for vehicle and home office deductions
- Contracts or invoices for service providers

Section 4: Penalties for False Deductions

Claiming false or exaggerated deductions results in:
- Disallowance of the improper deduction
- Reassessment of tax owed plus interest
- Penalty of 10-40% of the underpaid tax amount
- Potential criminal prosecution for fraud
- Mandatory audits for the next 5 years

Section 5: High Deduction Ratio Review Process

When deduction ratios exceed normal ranges, taxpayers must provide:
- Detailed explanation of each major deduction category
- Complete documentation for all deductions over €1,000
- Business records proving income and expense correlation
- Third-party verification for large deductions
- Comparative analysis with industry standards

Failure to provide adequate documentation results in automatic disallowance of excessive deductions.

Section 6: Industry-Specific Deduction Limits

Certain industries have specific deduction guidelines:
- IT consultants: Maximum 40% home office deduction
- Sales professionals: Vehicle expenses capped at €0.30 per km
- Healthcare professionals: Medical equipment depreciation over 5-10 years
- Artists and freelancers: Project-specific expense correlation required
- Real estate agents: Entertainment expenses limited to 70% deductibility

Section 7: Red Flag Indicators

The following patterns indicate potential deduction abuse:
- Sudden increase in deductions year-over-year (>30%)
- Deductions claimed in round numbers (suggesting estimates)
- Missing receipts for deductions over €250
- Deductions inconsistent with reported income level
- Business losses claimed for 3+ consecutive years
- Home office deduction for full-time office employees

Section 8: Voluntary Correction Program

Taxpayers who identify errors in claimed deductions may voluntarily correct their declarations within 4 years. Benefits include:
- Reduced penalties (50% reduction)
- No criminal prosecution
- Avoidance of mandatory future audits
- Installment payment options for amounts owed

Section 9: Professional Tax Advisor Responsibility

Tax advisors who consistently prepare declarations with excessive deductions face:
- Professional license review
- Liability for client penalties
- Mandatory quality audits
- Potential license suspension or revocation

Section 10: Legitimate High Deduction Scenarios

Certain circumstances justify high deduction ratios:
- Start-up business expenses in first 3 years
- Major business equipment purchases or renovations
- Relocation expenses for job-related moves
- Significant medical expenses with chronic illness documentation
- Casualty losses from disasters
- Alimony and child support payments

These scenarios require comprehensive documentation and may still trigger review procedures.
